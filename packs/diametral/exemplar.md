# Exemplar — `business-presentation.pptx` (51 slides, EN)

The **Diametral business presentation used in real prospection meetings**, and the
only finished, approved deck in this repo. `assets/template.pptx` shows what the
slots _are_; this shows what a good deck actually _says_ once they are filled.

**Look before you read**: the four contact sheets next to this file render the
deck — [s1–s9](exemplar-s1-s9.jpg), [s10–s18](exemplar-s10-s18.jpg),
[s19–s27](exemplar-s19-s27.jpg), [appendix](exemplar-appendix.jpg). They travel
with the packaged skill; the 21 MB deck itself does not (see §5). It lives at
`shared/slides/examples/business-presentation.pptx` in the marketplace repo, for
when you need to open a slide rather than look at it.

Read it when you have to decide **what goes on a slide**, not where the shape is
(that is [template-map.md](template-map.md)) and not how to draw a
bespoke composition (that is [compositions.md](compositions.md)).

> **Imitate the moves, never the content.** The copy below is Diametral's own
> pitch. Reusing its sentences in a client deck produces an off-topic deck that
> merely sounds right. What transfers is the _shape_ of each move: how long a
> title runs, what an eyebrow carries, how a card row is cut, where a number is
> allowed to be huge.

The commercial figures on the offering slides (day rates, mission magnitudes) are
deliberately **not reproduced here** — they date fast and they are not what makes
those slides work. Open the deck when you need the real values.

---

## 1 · The spine

The deck is a **meeting agenda made visible**, not a company brochure. Four acts,
each opened by a divider that states the act's question _and its budget in
minutes_ — the prospect always knows where they are and how long it lasts.

| Act              | Slides | Divider question        | Time | What it has to achieve                           |
| ---------------- | ------ | ----------------------- | ---- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 · Who we are   | s3–10  | "Who is Diametral?"     | 7'   | Earn the right to ask questions                  |
| 2 · Your context | s11–17 | "Why are we here?"      | 30'  | **Hand the floor over** — the longest act by far |
| 3 · What fits    | s18–23 | "What's your best fit?" | 10'  | Match the offer to what was just heard           |
| 4 · Next step    | s24–26 | "What's next?"          | 5'   | Close on a choice, not on a promise              |

Two structural decisions worth stealing:

1. **The client's act is the biggest one** (30' of a ~52' meeting). Acts 1 and 3
   exist to frame it. A deck that spends 30 minutes on itself has the ratio
   backwards.
2. **The appendix (s28–51) is a separate deck** behind one `Appendix` divider,
   with its own 6-chapter summary. Everything that is _true but not needed to
   advance this meeting_ lives there — method detail, case studies, proof points.
   The main line stays 27 slides.

## 2 · Act by act

### s1 · Cover — a promise, not a subject

> **Welcome to the new era of your performance.** (59 pt)
> `Business presentation` · `07/2026`

The cover title is a **sentence addressed to the reader** ("your performance"),
not a topic label ("Business presentation 2026" — that is demoted to the 12 pt
label). One line, ends with a full stop.

### s2 · Summary — numbered, 5 entries, no page numbers

`01 Diametral in a nutshell · 02 Your needs and stakes · 03 Your tailored
approach · 04 Our next steps · 05 Appendix`

Entries are **the promise of each act**, phrased from the client's side ("Your
needs and stakes", not "Discovery"). No page numbers — a summary that has to be
maintained is a summary that goes stale.

### s3 / s11 / s18 / s24 · Dividers — question + duration

```
01                     ← act number (32 pt)
Introduction           ← act kind
Diametral in a nutshell ← what the act covers
Who is Diametral?      ← THE QUESTION (50 pt)
7’                     ← the time it will take
```

The large type is always **a question**, never a noun phrase. The duration is the
move that makes this deck feel like a meeting rather than a presentation.

### s4 · Proof of footprint — figures carry it

> **A footprint close to our clients, with major successes.**
> `2` continents · `153` experts · `312` successes · `2018` since, each under one
> explanatory line ("Recognised expertise, historically as LittleBigCode."), over a
> strip of client logos.

Pattern: **big numeral (60–66 pt) + tiny unit label + one explanatory line**, four
across the band. The title states the conclusion; the figures are the evidence and
the logo strip is the proof. Nothing else on the slide.

### s5–s8 · The argument — four beats, one full-bleed image each

Each of these is the same composition (left photo panel, right text column, 9 pt
eyebrow above a 23 pt statement) carrying one beat of a chain:

| Slide | Eyebrow             | The beat                                                                                         |
| ----- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| s5    | `MISSION STATEMENT` | "Diametral crafts competitiveness of enterprises for the AI era."                                |
| s6    | `OUR CONVICTION`    | "The stake around AI is structural, not only technological." — with a sourced stat as a footnote |
| s7    | `THE REALITY`       | "AI isn't magical. It does not fix issues, nor does it generate value on its own."               |
| s8    | `VALUE FOR YOU`     | "A hands-on partner at your side to give life to your performance at the AI era."                |

Copy mechanics to steal:

- **The eyebrow types the slide** (`OUR CONVICTION`, `THE REALITY`) — 9 pt, all
  caps, ≤ ~20 chars. It tells the reader what kind of claim is coming.
- **The statement is 1–2 short lines**, then a blank line, then 2–4 lines of
  support. Never a paragraph straight after the title.
- **s7 argues in parallel clauses** — four sentences all starting "AI …", each
  taking one property away. Rhythm does the persuading.
- **s6 carries its source inline** ("Source: MIT"). An external number without a
  source is a liability in a prospection meeting.

### s9 · Eight engines — 8 cells, three text levels

`01 Strategy — Vision, alignment, prioritization.` … through `08`.

Numbered cell + 9 pt name + **6 pt three-word gloss**. Eight items only works
because each gloss is three nouns, not a sentence. If the gloss needs a verb, the
grid is too dense — cut to four.

### s10 / s19 · The three offers — the deck's recurring motif

`01 Expertise · 02 Process · 03 Immersion`, each with:

```
DIAMETRAL
Expertise                                    ← 18 pt
Our Data & AI experts from our 8 engines…    ← 8 pt, one sentence
USP    | A qualified set up ready to start in 5 days.
FORMAT | One-off project or run reinforcement.
```

**Labelled rows inside a card** (`USP |`, `FORMAT |`) let three cards stay
comparable — the eye reads across the row, not down the card. The same three
cards return on s19, s20 and s26 with different rows; repetition is the point,
the prospect learns the vocabulary once.

### s12–s14 · The discovery grid — the deck hands over the floor

The core move of act 2. Three consecutive slides that are **questions with
options**, not statements:

| Slide | Title                                                | Grid                                                                            |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| s12   | "What are you looking for? What do you need?"        | 3×3, grouped `FOR YOU` / `FOR YOUR COMPANY` / `FOR THE STAFF`                   |
| s13   | "What is your objective? What do you want to reach?" | 5 cells: Growth · Productivity · Transformation · Risk & Compliance · **Other** |
| s14   | "What are the main forces pushing you to transform?" | 5 cells: Urgency · Legacy · Resources · Transmission · **Other**                |

Two things make these work:

- **Every option is written from the client's mouth**: "You want to accelerate
  growth and get ahead of your market." Second person, present tense, one line.
- **There is always an `Other` cell.** It signals the list is a prompt, not a
  taxonomy the prospect must fit into.

### s15–s17 · The three ingredients — a numbered sub-series

`INGREDIENT 1 / 2 / 3` with a question title each (KPI, holistic view, enablers).
Numbering a sub-series inside an act tells the prospect how many more are coming.
Note **s17 is deliberately near-empty** — eyebrow and question only. It is a
whiteboard slide, filled live in the meeting.

### s20 · The comparison — five labelled rows across three offers

`PERFECT FIT FOR` · `MAIN OBJECTIVE` · `WHAT'S DELIVERED` · `VALUE PROPOSITION` ·
`HOW IT RUNS`. The densest slide of the main line, and it holds because every
cell answers the **same question** for each column. `HOW IT RUNS` renders its
value as a 4-step arrow chain (`Design → Build → Scale → Run`).

### s21–s23 · Offer detail — the FORMAT / DURATION / MAGNITUDE triplet

One slide per offer family, three formats per slide, each with an
`ILLUSTRATION` / `FORMAT` / `DURATION` / `MAGNITUDE` stack under a 20 pt name and
a one-line definition. **A commercial slide answers "how is it bought?" in a
fixed set of fields** — pricing model, duration, order of magnitude — so three
formats can be compared at a glance.

### s25 · The pivot — one sentence, nothing else

> `YOUR DECISION`
> **The question is not whether there is time to wait. The question is where to start.**

24 pt, a full-bleed image, no support text. Immediately before the ask. A deck
earns one of these; two would spend the effect.

### s26 · The close — three doors, not one ask

`First read.` / `First view.` / `First experience.` — one per offer family, each
with `FORMAT` / `YOUR BENEFIT` / `VALUE PROPOSITION`, and each low-commitment.

The title is **"What we propose as next step. What is your choice?"** The close
is a choice among three, so the answer is which one, not yes/no.

### s27 · "Thanks for listening!"

The template's closing slide, with `watching` → `listening` — it is a meeting,
not a broadcast. Localise the closing line only if asked.

## 3 · The appendix (s28–51) — what a proof library looks like

Behind one `Appendix` divider with its own 6-chapter summary. Useful patterns:

- **s31 · Anti-claims** — `We don't make slides` → "We deliver operational
  systems." Three pairs. Negation first, commitment second; far stronger than
  three positive claims.
- **s41 · The analogy slide** — a labelled diagram of a racing car (Engine, Tyre,
  Chassis, Driver, …) under "If you had to transform a basic car into a racing
  car, what would be your KPI and what would you start with?". A borrowed
  physical system explaining an abstract one.
- **s42 · Before / Now** — two columns, one word each: `Emails → Agents`,
  `Meetings → Automated decisions`, `Spreadsheets → Continuous data`. Six pairs,
  no sentences.
- **s43 · Three precedents** — `RETAIL → E-COMMERCE` / `TAXIS → MOBILITY` /
  `RECORDS → STREAMING`, each one line ("Traditional taxis weren't built to
  become Uber."). Same sentence frame three times.
- **s46 · Case studies as a failure narrative** — each case runs
  _what we believed_ → _what we did_ → _what actually happened_ → **the lesson**
  ("Speed up the intake without reinforcing the outflow, and you just create a
  traffic jam."). The case admits a mistake; that is why it reads as evidence
  rather than advertising.
- **s47–s48 · The single-number slide** — `5'51"` and `40%` at **112 pt**, a
  16 pt gloss, then one 10 pt line of consequence. Nothing else on the canvas.
  Used twice in 51 slides.

## 4 · Rules this deck confirms

Distilled, and consistent with [authoring-doctrine.md](authoring-doctrine.md):

1. **Content titles are one line and read as a verdict** — "AI isn't magical.",
   "Optimise one piece, and you just move the bottleneck." Statements with full
   stops, not labels.
2. **Every content slide carries a 9 pt all-caps eyebrow** naming the kind of
   claim. It is the slide's type signature.
3. **Speak in the second person.** Count the "your" in the section titles: the
   client is the subject, Diametral is the verb.
4. **One idea per slide, and a big number gets the whole canvas.** The two 112 pt
   slides work because nothing shares the page.
5. **Repeat a structure to teach it.** The three offers appear four times with
   different row labels; by s26 the prospect reads the card without instructions.
6. **Put the questions on the slides.** In a discovery deck, the slides that
   matter most are the ones the client answers.
7. **Everything optional goes in the appendix.** 27 slides of spine, 24 of proof.

## 5 · Using it in a build

- Never clone from this file's deck — it is an example, not a source. Clone from
  `assets/template.pptx`, which is the only sanctioned source of slides.
- The **deck** is not bundled into Cowork zips: at ~21 MB it would push a packaged
  skill past the upload limit. This Markdown and the four contact sheets (~1 MB
  total) are what travel with the skills — regenerate them with
  `soffice --headless --convert-to pdf` + `pdftoppm -r 96` if the deck is replaced.
- The English copy here is the deck's own; it sets **no default output language**
  (brand.md → Language). Write the deck in the language the request calls for.
