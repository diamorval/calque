# Narrative archetypes — starter catalog (v1, interim)

Five narrative archetypes keyed to the routing tree's **tone × angle** pair. Pick the best
fit, then adapt the beats live with the Business Manager — the skeleton is a starting point,
never a form to fill.

> **Interim catalog.** This file is superseded by the knowledge-base storyline catalog once
> the base is structured (named owner + contribution routine). Until then it is the only
> sanctioned source of archetypes — do not improvise new ones mid-session; extend this file.

Every beat names the **evidence it needs**. Deck-type depth applies to all archetypes:
Scaffold = sector-level evidence, Hybrid = mixed with named gaps, Rich = client-specific
throughout.

---

## 1 · Why AI, why now

**Fits:** tone **Educate** (no initiatives) · angle Growth or Cost-out · a room that has not
started and needs a reason to.

| #   | Beat                                    | Evidence needed                                           |
| --- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Your market is moving                   | block 03 trend + block 02 regional market figure          |
| 2   | Your peers already act on it            | block 03 / 05 market pains, peer moves (industry pattern) |
| 3   | What it means for your métier           | block 04 function × block 05 market pains                 |
| 4   | Where the value would sit for you       | block 02 client-vs-market verdict + block 05 client pains |
| 5   | A first step that does not bet the farm | maturity-appropriate scope; block 07/09 stated objectives |
| 6   | Proof it works                          | case library (or named gap until the connector exists)    |
| 7   | Next step                               | block 09 agreed next steps, or a proposal for the room    |

## 3 · Growth, compounded by data

## 4 · Run it right (governance & ROI)

**Fits:** angle **Risk** (regulatory / risk trigger) — **any tone**: Risk routes here
regardless of maturity ("angle wins over tone"). Natural fit is **Optimise** (at scale);
for an Educate or Accelerate room, beat 1 becomes "what the regulation expects of you"
(block 03 regulatory trends) instead of "what you run today".

| #   | Beat                                               | Evidence needed                               |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | What you run today                                 | block 06 signals                              |
| 2   | The pressure: regulation and risk are catching up  | block 03 regulatory trends + block 08 trigger |
| 3   | The cost of ungoverned AI                          | block 05 pains + industry pattern             |
| 4   | Governance as an operating model, not a brake      | Diametral positioning (`strategy.md`)         |
| 5   | Return-on-investment discipline: measure what runs | block 02 figures as the baseline              |
| 6   | Proof                                              | case library (or named gap)                   |
| 7   | Next step                                          | block 09 / proposal                           |

---

## Choosing between archetypes

1. **Angle wins over tone** when they conflict (the `pipeline.md` tie-break) — the trigger is
   why the client took the meeting; the tone adjusts how beats 1–3 are pitched, not which
   story is told.
2. **One archetype per deck.** If two fit, present the choice to the Business Manager with a
   one-line trade-off; never blend skeletons.
3. **Record the runner-up** and why it lost — the storyline document keeps that reasoning.

# Deck-routing decision tree

The full routing tree for a Diametral presale deck, transcribed from the internal framing
diagram ("Full routing decision tree for Diametral pre-sales deck", June 2026). The category
definitions live in `pipeline.md` — this file is the walk. Walk the questions **in order**;
every answer cites its source. A missing prereq is a question to the
Business Manager, never an assumption.

## Q1 — What do we already have?

_The starting point for everything that follows. Source: the material state of the
account — what actually exists — confirmed by the Business Manager._

| Answer                            | Knowledge level | Meaning                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nothing                           | **Cold start**  | Sector only — research first. **Do this first:** public sources, LinkedIn, annual report (i.e. run `first-meeting-brief`), then continue. |
| Meetings done, no account plan    | **Warm start**  | Context exists, gaps to fill.                                                                                                             |
| Full account plan + meeting notes | **Hot start**   | Rich context, personalise deep.                                                                                                           |

All paths converge on the questions below; the knowledge level is kept and reused at Q6.

## Q2 — What is the client's sector?

_Source: brief block 01 (account snapshot / account plan)._

Retail · Finance · Banque · Assurance · Industrie · Pharma · Services

The list comes from the source diagram and is **illustrative, not closed**: when the client
fits none of it (a beauty or consumer-goods group, an energy utility…), name the real sector
and use that — the sector feeds vocabulary, examples, and credible pains, not a lookup table.

## Q3 — Who is in the room?

_Source: brief block 04 (target person & function)._

HR · Finance · Commerce · Ops · IT · Direction / COMEX

(Q2's "Finance" is the client's **sector**; Q3's "Finance" is the **function** of the person
in the room — a Finance function exists in any sector. Sector names are kept verbatim from
the source diagram, French included.)

→ **Combination locked: sector × function.** These two fix the vocabulary, the examples, and
which pains from block 03/05 are credible in the room.

## Q4 — What is their AI maturity?

_Source: brief block 06 (AI initiatives), corrected by the debrief if a call happened._

Map the block-06 verdict to the **tone** per `pipeline.md` (No initiatives → Educate ·
POCs underway → Accelerate · At scale → Optimise).

## Q5 — What triggered the meeting?

_Source: brief block 08 (commercial context, filled by `first-touch-debrief`). If block 08 is
still a placeholder and no debrief note exists, ask the Business Manager._

Map the trigger to the **angle** per `pipeline.md` (Cost pressure → Cost-out · Growth target →
Growth · Regulatory / risk → Risk). An open trigger stays open — ask the room for it.

## Q6 — Deck type (by the Q1 knowledge level)

Map the knowledge level to the **deck type and evidence depth** per `pipeline.md`
(Cold → Scaffold · Warm → Hybrid, name the gaps · Hot → Rich).

## Routing record

The walk ends with six answers — knowledge level, sector, function, tone, angle, deck type —
each with its source. That record opens the storyline document; the archetype is chosen from
the tone × angle pair (see `archetypes.md`).
