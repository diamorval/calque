# Narrative archetypes — starter catalog (v1, interim)

Seven narrative archetypes: five pre-sales arcs keyed to the routing tree's **tone × angle**
pair, one event or webinar arc, one offer-launch arc. Pick the best fit, then adapt the beats live with the Business Manager — the skeleton is a starting point,
never a form to fill.

> **Interim catalog.** This file is superseded by the knowledge-base storyline catalog once
> the base is structured (named owner + contribution routine). Until then it is the only
> sanctioned source of archetypes — do not improvise new ones mid-session; extend this file.

Every beat names the **evidence it needs**. Deck-type depth applies to all archetypes:
Scaffold = sector-level evidence, Hybrid = mixed with named gaps, Rich = client-specific
throughout.

**Which part of this file applies.** Archetypes 1–5 and the routing tree are for a **pre-sales
meeting deck** (one client in the room). A deck for an **event or webinar** (an audience of
many companies) uses archetype 6; a deck **launching an offer** (to the market, a partner or
the sales team) uses archetype 7. Neither of those walks the routing tree.

## Reading the evidence references

Archetypes 1–5 cite the **pre-meeting brief**, a nine-block research dossier on the prospect
(drafted by the `first-meeting-brief` workflow, blocks 08–09 completed after the first call by
`first-touch-debrief`). It is not part of this pack. **If the brief exists**, read the block
named; **if it does not**, the reference tells you what evidence to look for in the material you
have, and a beat with none becomes a named gap (`[À COMPLÉTER]` / `[TO COMPLETE]`), never an
invented fact.

| Block | Holds                                                                                                |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01    | Account snapshot: the company, its sector, size, structure, any existing account plan                |
| 02    | Figures: turnover and business-unit trends, regional market figures, the client-vs-market verdict    |
| 03    | Sector trends: industry patterns, what peers do, regulatory trends                                   |
| 04    | The person in the room: role, function, scope, priorities                                            |
| 05    | Pains: those of the market and those specific to the client                                          |
| 06    | AI initiatives: public signals of the client's data and AI work, and the maturity verdict            |
| 07    | Meeting hypotheses: the angle we propose and the objectives we expect the client to hold             |
| 08    | Commercial context, after the first call: the trigger of the meeting                                 |
| 09    | Debrief of the first call: key quotes, stated objectives, agreed next steps                          |

Other references: **Diametral positioning** (`strategy.md`, outside the pack: what Diametral
sells and how it differs; without it, use `exemplar.md` and the voice's four profiles), the
**case library** (client references; until it is connected, the proof beat is a named gap), and
`pipeline.md` (the tone and angle definitions, summarised in the routing tree below).

## 1 · Why AI, why now

**Fits:** tone **Educate** (no initiatives) · angle Growth or Cost-out · a room that has not
started and needs a reason to.

| #   | Beat                                    | Evidence needed                                           |
| --- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Your market is moving                   | block 03 trend + block 02 regional market figure          |
| 2   | Your peers already act on it            | block 03 / 05 market pains, peer moves (industry pattern) |
| 3   | What it means for your métier           | block 04 function × block 05 market pains                 |
| 4   | Where the value would sit for you       | block 02 client-vs-market verdict + block 05 client pains |
| 5   | A first step that does not bet the farm | maturity-appropriate scope; block 07/09 stated objectives |
| 6   | Proof it works                          | case library (or named gap until the connector exists)    |
| 7   | Next step                               | block 09 agreed next steps, or a proposal for the room    |

## 2 · From POC to production

**Fits:** tone **Accelerate** (POCs underway) · angle Cost-out or Growth · a room stuck in
pilot purgatory.

| #   | Beat                                                    | Evidence needed                         |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 1   | You have already started — credit where due             | block 06 signals                        |
| 2   | The POC-to-production gap is the industry's problem too | block 03 (industry pattern)             |
| 3   | What blocks scale here                                  | block 05 client pains + block 09 quotes |
| 4   | The operating model that crosses the gap                | Diametral positioning (`strategy.md`)   |
| 5   | What production-grade unlocks, in figures               | block 02 figures reframed               |
| 6   | Proof                                                   | case library (or named gap)             |
| 7   | Next step                                               | block 09 / proposal                     |

## 3 · Growth, compounded by data

**Fits:** angle **Growth** (growth-target trigger) · tone Accelerate or Optimise · a room
chasing a revenue target.

| #   | Beat                                                     | Evidence needed                                   |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | The target, as you stated it                             | block 08 trigger + block 09 stated objectives     |
| 2   | Where the market grows — and whether you keep pace       | block 02 client-vs-market verdict                 |
| 3   | The growth levers data & AI actually move in your métier | block 03 × block 04 function                      |
| 4   | Your two or three highest-yield use cases                | block 05 client pains inverted into opportunities |
| 5   | Proof                                                    | case library (or named gap)                       |
| 6   | The path: sequence and first win                         | maturity-appropriate roadmap (block 06)           |
| 7   | Next step                                                | block 09 / proposal                               |

## 4 · Run it right (governance & ROI)

**Fits:** angle **Risk** (regulatory / risk trigger) — **any tone**: Risk routes here
regardless of maturity ("angle wins over tone"). Natural fit is **Optimise** (at scale);
for an Educate or Accelerate room, beat 1 becomes "what the regulation expects of you"
(block 03 regulatory trends) instead of "what you run today".

| #   | Beat                                               | Evidence needed                               |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | What you run today                                 | block 06 signals                              |
| 2   | The pressure: regulation and risk are catching up  | block 03 regulatory trends + block 08 trigger |
| 3   | The cost of ungoverned AI                          | block 05 pains + industry pattern             |
| 4   | Governance as an operating model, not a brake      | Diametral positioning (`strategy.md`)         |
| 5   | Return-on-investment discipline: measure what runs | block 02 figures as the baseline              |
| 6   | Proof                                              | case library (or named gap)                   |
| 7   | Next step                                          | block 09 / proposal                           |

## 5 · Efficiency under pressure

**Fits:** angle **Cost-out** (cost-pressure trigger) · any tone — the maturity sets how bold
beat 4 can be.

| #   | Beat                                                           | Evidence needed                                              |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | The pressure, in your figures                                  | block 08 trigger + block 02 turnover and business-unit trend |
| 2   | Where your métier bleeds                                       | block 05 client pains, ranked                                |
| 3   | What peers under the same pressure did                         | block 03 (industry pattern)                                  |
| 4   | The efficiency levers, ranked by feasibility for your maturity | block 06 verdict × block 05                                  |
| 5   | Quantified ambition — honest ranges, sourced baselines         | block 02; never an invented saving                           |
| 6   | Proof                                                          | case library (or named gap)                                  |
| 7   | Next step                                                      | block 09 / proposal                                          |

## 6 · Event or webinar: one finding, shared

**Fits:** a talk to an audience of many companies (conference slot, webinar, client breakfast,
plenary). No brief, no routing tree: the audience is a population, so evidence is sector-level
by construction. The deck teaches one finding; the offer appears once, at the end, as the next
step for those who want it.

| #   | Beat                                                | Evidence needed                                                        |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | The finding, stated as a verdict                    | the event's theme + one sourced figure (study, survey, our own data)   |
| 2   | Why it matters to this audience now                 | sector trend, regulatory date or market figure, each with its source   |
| 3   | What the field shows                                | 2–3 anonymised observations from engagements, or a named public case   |
| 4   | The mechanism: why it happens                       | our method or framework, in builder's vocabulary, no product pitch     |
| 5   | What to do on Monday                                | 3–5 concrete actions the audience can take without us                  |
| 6   | Proof                                               | case library (or named gap), or a guest client's own words             |
| 7   | Next step                                           | the call to action of the event: a diagnostic, a workshop, a resource  |

Rules: one finding per talk; every figure carries its source on the slide; the speaker's
name and the event date go on the cover; a webinar keeps beat 5 on screen during questions.

## 7 · Offer launch: the problem, then the offer

**Fits:** a deck that launches or relaunches a Diametral offer, to the market (site, LinkedIn
carousel, event booth), to a partner, or to the sales team. The reader has no meeting with us
yet: the deck sells the problem before it names the offer.

| #   | Beat                                                    | Evidence needed                                                  |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | The problem the offer solves, in the buyer's words      | market pains from engagements or a sourced study                 |
| 2   | Why existing answers fall short                         | the market's usual approaches and where they stall (no names)    |
| 3   | The offer: what it is, in one sentence                  | the offer sheet: name, scope, format, duration                   |
| 4   | How it runs: the steps and what each delivers           | the offer's method, step by step, with its deliverables          |
| 5   | What the client gets, in figures                        | outcomes measured on engagements, as honest ranges with sources  |
| 6   | Proof                                                   | case library (or named gap)                                      |
| 7   | Price and entry point, or how to start                  | the offer sheet: pricing or the first step; for sales, the pitch |

Rules: the offer's name appears from beat 3, never in the title of beats 1–2; an internal
sales-team version adds, after beat 7, the target accounts and the objections with their
answers; the launch date and the offer owner go in the speaker notes.

## Choosing between archetypes

1. **Angle wins over tone** when they conflict (the `pipeline.md` tie-break) — the trigger is
   why the client took the meeting; the tone adjusts how beats 1–3 are pitched, not which
   story is told.
2. **The audience picks the family first**: one client in the room → 1–5; an audience of
   many companies → 6; an offer reaching people who have not met us → 7.
3. **One archetype per deck.** If two fit, present the choice to the Business Manager with a
   one-line trade-off; never blend skeletons.
4. **Record the runner-up** and why it lost — the storyline document keeps that reasoning.

# Deck-routing decision tree

The full routing tree for a Diametral presale deck, transcribed from the internal framing
diagram ("Full routing decision tree for Diametral pre-sales deck", June 2026). The category
definitions live in `pipeline.md` — this file is the walk. Walk the questions **in order**;
every answer cites its source. A missing prereq is a question to the
Business Manager, never an assumption.

## Q1 — What do we already have?

_The starting point for everything that follows. Source: the material state of the
account — what actually exists — confirmed by the Business Manager._

| Answer                            | Knowledge level | Meaning                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nothing                           | **Cold start**  | Sector only — research first. **Do this first:** public sources, LinkedIn, annual report (i.e. run `first-meeting-brief`), then continue. |
| Meetings done, no account plan    | **Warm start**  | Context exists, gaps to fill.                                                                                                             |
| Full account plan + meeting notes | **Hot start**   | Rich context, personalise deep.                                                                                                           |

All paths converge on the questions below; the knowledge level is kept and reused at Q6.

## Q2 — What is the client's sector?

_Source: brief block 01 (account snapshot / account plan)._

Retail · Finance · Banque · Assurance · Industrie · Pharma · Services

The list comes from the source diagram and is **illustrative, not closed**: when the client
fits none of it (a beauty or consumer-goods group, an energy utility…), name the real sector
and use that — the sector feeds vocabulary, examples, and credible pains, not a lookup table.

## Q3 — Who is in the room?

_Source: brief block 04 (target person & function)._

HR · Finance · Commerce · Ops · IT · Direction / COMEX

(Q2's "Finance" is the client's **sector**; Q3's "Finance" is the **function** of the person
in the room — a Finance function exists in any sector. Sector names are kept verbatim from
the source diagram, French included.)

→ **Combination locked: sector × function.** These two fix the vocabulary, the examples, and
which pains from block 03/05 are credible in the room.

## Q4 — What is their AI maturity?

_Source: brief block 06 (AI initiatives), corrected by the debrief if a call happened._

Map the block-06 verdict to the **tone** per `pipeline.md` (No initiatives → Educate ·
POCs underway → Accelerate · At scale → Optimise).

## Q5 — What triggered the meeting?

_Source: brief block 08 (commercial context, filled by `first-touch-debrief`). If block 08 is
still a placeholder and no debrief note exists, ask the Business Manager._

Map the trigger to the **angle** per `pipeline.md` (Cost pressure → Cost-out · Growth target →
Growth · Regulatory / risk → Risk). An open trigger stays open — ask the room for it.

## Q6 — Deck type (by the Q1 knowledge level)

Map the knowledge level to the **deck type and evidence depth** per `pipeline.md`
(Cold → Scaffold · Warm → Hybrid, name the gaps · Hot → Rich).

## Routing record

The walk ends with six answers — knowledge level, sector, function, tone, angle, deck type —
each with its source. That record opens the storyline document; the archetype is chosen from
the tone × angle pair (archetypes 1–5 above).
