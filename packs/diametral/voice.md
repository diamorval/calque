# Diametral voice — core rules (digest)

> **Derived** from [`writing-rules.md`](writing-rules.md) + [`tone-of-voice.md`](tone-of-voice.md)
> (Confluence-governed). The **complete set of hard rules**, compressed for generation-time use:
> read this before writing any Diametral copy. Open the full files for the worked ❌/✅ examples,
> long-form prose, or contested calls. Never edit this file to add a rule — update the source
> files first, then re-derive this digest.

**Why the rules exist:** Diametral sells structural work. Copy that reads like every other
consultancy undercuts the claim before the reader reaches the offer. Each rule removes a specific
tell.

**Scope:** documents, emails, posts, proposals, reports, presentations, deliverables — anything
client-facing or public. **Not** source code, raw data output, or chat with the user.

## Naming

- Always **"Diametral"** — capital D, nothing else. Never DIAMETRAL / Diamétral / diametral.
- Always **"AI-native"** — hyphenated, lowercase after the hyphen. Never "AI native", "AI-Native" or "AI-First".

## Hard mechanics — every text, no exception

- **Active voice.** The subject acts, it is never acted upon.
- **One idea per sentence.** Short sentences; cut overly long subordinate clauses.
- **Every sentence ends with a period — titles included.**
- **No em-dash "—"**, no middle dot as a separator, no deep bullet nesting — all AI-writing markers; rewrite as two sentences. A dash **between two numbers in a range** is correct: keep it.
- **No ellipsis "…", no "!"** — anywhere, titles included.
- **Avoid parentheses** — use a semicolon or split into two sentences.
- **No italics** except foreign-language terms; **quotation marks** only for direct quotes and self-referential terms (no scare quotes).
- **No mid-sentence capitals** except proper nouns (companies, tools, technologies, people).
- **Zero "etc."** — a list is exhaustive or closed.
- **Positive assertion.** Say what we are, never what we don't do.
- **Nominal titles** — noun-based, verdict-like: _"Data mesh: a structural decision."_
- **Tenses**: simple present; avoid progressives ("we are currently working on"). **Imperative** for calls to action.
- **Pronouns**: "we" = Diametral's engineers and architects; "you" = the client. Keep the roles distinct.
- **No emojis in anything client-facing** — proposals, reports, emails, presentations, deliverables.

## Cadence — the tells that survive a clean grammar pass

- **Vary sentence length.** Uniform rhythm is the clearest signal of generated text.
- **No antithesis construction**: "not just X, it is Y" / "it is not about X, it is about Y". It performs insight instead of carrying it.
- **No three-item list for rhythm.** Three items carry three facts, or the list becomes two.
- **No hollow intensifiers**: genuinely, truly, deeply, incredibly, remarkably, powerful, seamless, robust. State the mechanism; let the reader draw the conclusion.
- **Open on a statement**, never a rhetorical question. **Close on the last substantive point**, never a summarizing flourish.

## Register

- **Intelligible, not "simple".** Complex subjects, absolute clarity; define technical terms rather than dodge them.
- **Builder's vocabulary**: foundations, base, model, engineering, flows, architecture, system.
- **Ban buzzwords**: if any startup could say it, it has no place at Diametral.

## Language

Write in the language of the request. There is no default language: it is the user's call, every
time. A French name inside the content does not make the request French. Where the request does not
settle it, ask which language before writing.

## The 4 tone profiles — embody at least one per piece

| Profile      | Quality       | Write like                                                                                                                |
| ------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Builder**  | Visionary     | Talk organisation and systems, not tools. Present tense. Sober. Ban "revolution" → _transformation, overhaul, alignment_. |
| **Revealer** | Crystal-clear | Short sentences. Cut jargon. State the finding directly. No extended metaphors.                                           |
| **Pillar**   | Expert        | Direct assertion (_"Here is the standard."_). No superlatives. Engineer's tone: factual, evidence-based. Own positions.   |
| **Achiever** | Pragmatic     | Action verbs: deliver, operate, test, deploy, manage. Ground-level facts. Close on the concrete business impact.          |

Guardrails: Visionary ≠ dreamy/futuristic/abstract · Crystal-clear ≠ simplistic/verbose/naïve · Expert ≠ arrogant/cold/professorial · Pragmatic ≠ hasty/blunt/sloppy.

## Banned words

- **Empty AI jargon**: AI-powered, Full-AI, disruptive, scalable (without saying how), Ecosystem 4.0, AI in the service of humanity, all-in-one solution.
- **False promises**: revolutionary, magic solution, limitless, the AI that replaces everything, futuristic, instant results.
- **Weak verbal tics**: we're here to help, don't worry, we handle everything, we'll walk you through it, it's as easy as that.
- **Filler buzzwords**: agile, caring, 360 solution.
- **Hollow intensifiers**: genuinely, truly, deeply, incredibly, remarkably, powerful, seamless, robust.

## Before you deliver — the five that catch most failures

1. Every sentence ends with a period, and no exclamation mark or ellipsis survived.
2. No em-dash, no parentheses, no scare quotes.
3. No hollow intensifier, no antithesis construction, no rhythm-of-three.
4. Active voice throughout, one idea per sentence.
5. "Diametral" and "AI-native" spelled correctly everywhere.

Then check the opening and the closing. The first sentence states something. The last sentence
carries content rather than summarizing what came before.

## Review mode

When a draft is pasted in for checking, do both jobs. List each violation with the rule it breaks
and the corrected line, then give the clean version in full. Order by how visible the violation is
to a client: brand-name errors and emojis first, cadence tells last. Never soften a violation into a
suggestion; the rules are the standard.

# Tone of voice — Diametral

> Source of truth: **Confluence → Brand → Brand Guidelines → Tone of Voice**
> (`COMMUNICAT`, page 2666496006), authored by the Marketing department (CMO Juliette Bechdolff).
> Faithful capture. Pair with [`writing-rules.md`](writing-rules.md) (the mechanics) and
> [`strategy.md`](strategy.md) (the positioning behind the voice).

The Tone of Voice defines **how Diametral sounds** — the personality and emotion behind every word.
Beyond grammar, it is the character we express: bold yet precise, expert yet accessible, forward-looking
yet grounded. Wherever the audience meets Diametral — website, pitch deck, kakemono — they feel the same
distinctive voice.

## The 4 qualities

Every piece of communication must embody **at least one** of these four qualities; the strongest
messages combine several at once.

| Quality                      | Referred to as   |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Visionary                    | the **Builder**  |
| Crystal-clear (_Cristallin_) | the **Revealer** |
| Expert                       | the **Pillar**   |
| Pragmatic                    | the **Achiever** |

> Note: the Confluence page labels the Pragmatic posture "the **Executor**" in its qualities table but
> "the **Achiever**" in the profile heading and body. We use **Achiever** throughout (the source's
> majority usage); treat the two as the same posture.

## The 4 profiles

Behind each quality stands a posture: the Builder shapes the vision, the Revealer makes it legible, the
Pillar ensures it holds, the Achiever makes it real.

### 1. The Builder — a sense of the system and of the bigger picture

_Philosophy: "Diametral is a brand that doesn't just anticipate the AI-native era. It builds it."_

We approach the company as a **living architecture**. Where others see isolated tools, we design complete
systems. Our vision is the one of an architect: we draw the blueprints for lasting performance, where
strategy and execution align perfectly to transform the organisation into a natively intelligent organism.

**What the audience should feel**

- _"They understand that for us, AI isn't a technical option. It's our future way of operating."_
- _"We're not talking about gadgets. We're talking about the structure of the business itself."_
- _"They have a holistic view that resets everything, from strategy down to code."_

**Visionary does NOT mean being:** ⚠️ dreamy, futuristic, abstract, disconnected, poetic, theoretical.

**Writing guidelines**

- **Talk organisation:** we don't sell AI, we sell a new way to run a business.
- **Use Data & AI-related terms:** prefer _model, flow, infrastructure, coherence, system._
- **Avoid the future tense:** speak in the present. It's not what AI will be: it's what it does to the
  business now. (This rule can flex for stylistic effect.)
- **Connect the dots:** always show the impact of a technical choice on the business.
- **Stay sober:** no grandiose adjectives. The solidity of the project speaks for itself.
- **Ban "revolution":** use _transformation, overhaul_ or _alignment_ instead.

### 2. The Revealer — the art of making complexity intelligible

_Philosophy: "Diametral is a brand that brings light where technical density reigns."_

Our role is to dispel the technological fog and make way for a **clear reading of reality**. Through
rigorous synthesis and a geometric approach to pain points, we translate the most complex data challenges
into self-evident business trajectories. We make the future readable; enabling decisions without ambiguity.

**What the audience should feel**

- _"This is the first time I truly understand where we're going and why."_
- _"They managed to explain the thing simply, without talking down to us."_
- _"Suddenly, the project just made sense."_

**Crystal-clear does NOT mean being:** ⚠️ simplistic, didactic, verbose, soft, forgettable, naïve.

**Writing guidelines**

- **The short sentence is a weapon:** if it's too long, you are not clear enough yet.
- **Cut unnecessary jargon:** if a word doesn't help understanding, remove it.
- **State the obvious:** don't beat around the bush. Go straight to the finding.
- **Use "anchored" words:** words that ground the subject — clarity, direction, finding, reading.
- **Answer before the question is asked:** anticipate the client's confusion or question.
- **Avoid extended metaphors:** an image should illuminate the point, not become the point.

### 3. The Pillar — the rigour of engineering and structure

_Philosophy: "Diametral is a brand that embodies the structural authority of data and AI."_

We are the **guarantor of your foundations' solidity**. As experts in architecture and technical
infrastructure, we enforce a mathematical, surgical rigour of method. For us, a foundation only succeeds
if it is robust, scalable, secured and industrialized. We are the square base on which your most ambitious
goals rest.

**What the audience should feel**

- _"You can tell they know their subject. It's incredibly tight."_
- _"It's reassuring to work with people who have that level of technical rigour and expertise."_
- _"They're not here to help us. They're here to make it work."_

**Expert does NOT mean being:** ⚠️ arrogant, cold, rigid, professorial, condescending, bureaucratic.

**Writing guidelines**

- **Direct assertion:** no "I think"; prefer _"Here is the standard"_ or _"This is how we do it."_
- **Talk method:** explain the how with surgical precision.
- **No superlatives:** _"effective"_ beats _"incredibly powerful."_
- **Use the engineer's tone:** precise, factual, grounded in evidence and real-world experience.
- **Embody solidity:** your text must be stable. No exclamation marks, no embellishment.
- **Own your positions:** an expert has a clear opinion on what is technically sound and what isn't.

### 4. The Achiever — an obsession with delivery and concrete transformation

_Philosophy: "Diametral is a brand that transforms technological intention into industrial and business
impact."_

We are the agents of the diagonal. The one that breaks inertia to create movement. Our pragmatism drives
us out of theoretical frameworks and onto the ground. We don't just deliver ideas. We **industrialise
performance**. For Diametral, the value of an AI-native strategy is measured by its capacity for execution
and its real effectiveness in production.

**What the audience should feel**

- _"With them, things move. You don't spend six months on slides."_
- _"What they deliver actually works in the field."_
- _"They're wired for performance, not theory."_

**Pragmatic does NOT mean being:** ⚠️ hasty, blunt, order-taking, short-termist, sloppy.

**Writing guidelines**

- **Pure action verbs:** deliver, operate, test, deploy, manage.
- **Results focus:** talk about what the end user will see or gain.
- **Talk ground-level:** reference real constraints, bugs, production, the reality of live systems.
- **Sustained rhythm:** use lists or steps. Show the project in motion.
- **Cut the strategic blabla:** we know why we're doing it. Now show how it gets done.
- **Proof through use:** always close with the concrete business impact.

## Words to avoid

Overused words weaken a brand's voice. They make the message less distinctive, less sincere. For every
word to count, here is what's best avoided.

| ❌ Empty AI jargon            | ❌ False promises               | ❌ Weak verbal tics       |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| AI-powered / Full-AI          | Revolutionary                   | We're here to help        |
| Disruptive                    | Magic solution                  | Don't worry               |
| Scalable (without saying how) | Limitless                       | We handle everything      |
| Ecosystem 4.0                 | The AI that replaces everything | We'll walk you through it |
| AI in the service of humanity | Futuristic                      | It's as easy as that      |
| All-in-one solution           | Instant results                 |                           |

# Writing rules — Diametral

> Source of truth: **Confluence → Brand → Brand Guidelines → Writing rules**
> (`COMMUNICAT`, page 2665316353), authored by the Marketing department (CMO Juliette Bechdolff).
> Faithful capture — apply without exception. Check that page when the user asks for the
> current revision; otherwise apply what follows.
> Pair with [`tone-of-voice.md`](tone-of-voice.md) (the personality behind the words) and
> [`messaging.md`](messaging.md) (ready-to-use copy). The compressed generation-time digest
> is [`voice-core.md`](voice-core.md).

The rules exist because Diametral sells structural work. Copy that reads like every other
consultancy undercuts the claim before the reader reaches the offer. Each rule below removes a
specific tell. Every rule is illustrated with what works ✅ and what does not ❌.

## Scope

Applies to: documents, emails, posts, proposals, reports, presentations, deliverables, anything
client-facing or public.

Does not apply to: source code, raw data output, and conversation in the chat window. Working
notes to the user stay normal.

## Name & keywords

**Name**

- ✅ **Diametral**: used every time the firm's name appears (business, presentation, legal documents,
  HR job descriptions). Capital D, no accent, no other variant. _"At Diametral, our mission is to…"_
- ❌ DIAMETRAL ❌ Diamétral ❌ diametral

**Mission**

- ✅ **AI-native**: hyphenated, lowercase after the hyphen. _"We are an AI-native company."_
- ❌ AI native — the unhyphenated form is no longer accepted.
- ❌ AI-Native ❌ AI-First — we have chosen "AI-native" to represent our mission.

## Editorial rules

### 1. Language register

- **Intelligibility over simplicity.** We don't aim to be "simple" (which can read as reductive), but
  intelligible. We address complex subjects with absolute clarity.
  > ❌ _"We make AI easy for everyone."_
  > ✅ _"We design data architectures that make machine learning models operable at scale."_
- **Builder's vocabulary.** Favour the lexical field of architecture and structure (foundations, base,
  model, engineering, flows).
  > ❌ _"We help you get the most out of your data."_
  > ✅ _"We lay the data foundations your decision systems run on."_
- **Ban filler words.** No buzzwords (agile, disruptive, caring, 360 solution). The test: if any
  startup could say the sentence, Diametral does not write it.
  > ❌ _"Our agile, disruptive approach delivers seamless AI solutions."_
  > ✅ _"We architect production-ready AI systems, from ingestion pipelines to inference layers."_
- **Technical precision.** Technical terms are permitted when they are the only way to be precise.
  Define the term rather than dilute it.
  > ❌ _"We clean up your data before using it."_
  > ✅ _"We run data quality audits before any modelling phase begins."_
- **Zero "etc."** Every list closes or it is exhaustive. "Etc." admits the thinking stopped early.
  > ❌ _"We work with data pipelines, dashboards, models, etc."_
  > ✅ _"We work with data pipelines, dashboards, and predictive models."_

**Typography (in text)**

- **No unauthorized capitals.** Do not capitalize words mid-sentence unless they are proper nouns
  (companies, tools, technologies, people).
  > ❌ _"We build Data Pipelines and Machine Learning Systems for our Clients."_
  > ✅ _"We build data pipelines and machine learning systems for our clients."_
  > ✅ _"We deploy models on Google Cloud and Azure."_ (proper nouns; correct)
- **No italics except for foreign-language terms.** Not for emphasis.
  > ❌ _"Our approach is truly_ unique._"_ (italics for emphasis)
  > ✅ _"The process follows an_ a posteriori _validation logic."_ (Latin term; correct)
- **No AI-typography markers.** The **em-dash "—"**, **middle dots used as separators**, and deep
  bullet nesting all read as automated writing rather than structured human thinking. Rewrite the
  sentence instead of reaching for the character.
  > ❌ _"We rebuilt the pipeline — handling batch and streaming — in under six weeks."_
  > ✅ _"We rebuilt the pipeline in under six weeks. It now handles both batch and streaming data."_
- **A dash between two numbers in a range is correct typography. Keep it.**
  > ✅ _"The migration ran over weeks 3–8."_

**Punctuation**

- **Every sentence ends with a period. Titles included.**
  > ❌ _"We design the backbone of your AI infrastructure"_
  > ✅ _"We design the backbone of your AI infrastructure."_
- **No ellipses. No exclamation marks. Anywhere, titles included.**
  > ❌ _"The model is live… and it's just the beginning!"_
  > ✅ _"The model is live. The next phase begins now."_
- **Avoid parentheses.** Parenthetical content announces itself as secondary, which means it should
  not be in the text. Use a semicolon, or split into two sentences.
  > ❌ _"The pipeline (which handles both batch and streaming data) was rebuilt from scratch."_
  > ✅ _"The pipeline was rebuilt from scratch; it now handles both batch and streaming data."_
- **Quotation marks for direct quotes and self-referential terms only.** No scare quotes around a
  word you want to lean on.
  > ❌ _Our approach is 'different'._ (scare quotes for vague emphasis; not permitted)
  > ✅ _"The term 'data lake' is often misused as a synonym for 'data warehouse'."_

### 2. Cadence

- **Vary sentence length.** Uniform rhythm is the clearest signal of generated text.
- **Never use the antithesis construction**: "not just X, it is Y", "it is not about X, it is about
  Y". It performs insight instead of carrying it.
- **Never use a three-item list for rhythm.** Three items carry three facts, or the list becomes two.
- **Cut hollow intensifiers**: genuinely, truly, deeply, incredibly, remarkably, powerful, seamless,
  robust. They assert quality the sentence has not earned. State the mechanism instead and let the
  reader draw the conclusion.
  > ❌ _"A genuinely robust, seamless ingestion layer."_
  > ✅ _"The ingestion layer absorbs a 40× traffic spike without backpressure."_
- **Open on a statement**, never on a rhetorical question.
- **Close on the last substantive point**, never on a summarizing flourish. When the content ends,
  stop.

### 3. Grammar

- **Active voice.** The golden rule. The subject acts. It is never acted upon.
  > ❌ _"The data architecture was designed by our engineers."_
  > ✅ _"Our engineers designed the data architecture."_
- **Density.** One idea per sentence. Cut long subordinate clauses; clarity comes from punctuation.
  > ❌ _"Because the client's legacy system was built on a monolithic architecture that had not been
  > updated since 2015, which made integration with modern APIs extremely complex, we recommended a
  > phased migration approach."_
  > ✅ _"The client's legacy system dated from 2015: monolithic, with no modern API layer. We recommended
  > a phased migration."_
- **Positive assertion.** State what Diametral is and does. A high-status brand does not define
  itself by what it avoids.
  > ❌ _"Your data will no longer be siloed or inaccessible."_
  > ✅ _"We unify your data into a single, queryable layer."_
- **Nominal titles.** Noun-based, with a verdict-like tone.
  > ❌ _"Why you should think about building a data mesh"_
  > ✅ _"Data mesh: a structural decision."_

### 4. Tenses and pronouns

- **Prefer the simple present.** Avoid progressive forms such as "we are currently working on".
  > ❌ _"We are currently working on redesigning your ingestion layer."_
  > ✅ _"We redesign your ingestion layer."_
- **Use the imperative** for calls to action.
  > ❌ _"You might want to consider auditing your data governance framework."_
  > ✅ _"Audit your data governance framework before scaling."_
- **Pronouns.** "We" designates Diametral's engineers and architects. "You" designates the client.
  Keep the two roles distinct.
  > ❌ _"Users can access their dashboards at any time."_ (impersonal; distances the reader)
  > ✅ _"You own the strategy. We engineer the infrastructure that makes it run."_

### 5. Visual

- **No emojis in any client-facing communication**: proposals, reports, emails, presentations, or any
  written deliverable. They undermine the authority and structural tone that defines Diametral.
  > ❌ _"Here is our proposal for your data infrastructure 🚀. We're excited to work with you! 💡"_
  > ✅ _"Please find attached our proposal for your data infrastructure."_

### 6. Language

Write in the language of the request. There is no default language: it is the user's call, every
time. A French name inside the content does not make the request French. Where the request does not
settle it, ask which language before writing.

2. No em-dash, no parentheses, no scare quotes.
3. No hollow intensifier, no antithesis construction, no rhythm-of-three.
4. Active voice throughout, one idea per sentence.
5. "Diametral" and "AI-native" spelled correctly everywhere.

Then check the opening and the closing. The first sentence states something. The last sentence
carries content rather than summarizing what came before.
